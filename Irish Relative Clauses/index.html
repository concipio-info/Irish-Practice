Quick reference: how do I decide?
Direct · a + lenitionHead noun is the subject or direct object of the relative verb. an bhean a phóg Gearóid can mean either “the woman who kissed Gearóid” or “the woman whom Gearóid kissed.”
Indirect · a + eclipsisThe head noun is linked through a preposition or possessive (or a repeated pronoun for clarity). an bhean a bhfuil cónaí orm léi. Dependent verb forms apply.
Indirect past · ar + lenitionMost regular verbs and five irregular verbs take ar: an bhean ar phóg Gearóid í. Six irregular verbs instead take a plus their dependent form: a bhfaca, a raibh.
Negative · nach / nárnach eclipses; past nár generally lenites. Six past irregular verbs keep nach: nach bhfaca, nach raibh.

Important: English “whom” alone does not force an indirect relative in Irish. A direct object can use a direct relative. Indirect wording may repeat a pronoun to remove ambiguity. A chur means “to put” and is not itself a relative particle.
