Learn the directions

Three different ideas: where you are going, where something is, and where something comes from. For example: suas = upwards; thuas = up above; anuas = down from above. Ó thuaidh means northwards here: do not interpret its ó as “from”.

Compass directions are fixed. Left and right depend on your facing direction. In the compass diagrams, English N/E/S/W labels anchor the map; a gold arrow shows your heading and a green arrow shows movement. Dots show locations. In origin exercises the arrow points towards the centre.

Language notes & sources

Exercises use standard teaching distinctions. In actual speech, directional forms can overlap and vary by dialect. Siar can also mean backwards; context determines the meaning. Ar ais means back/return, which need not mean walking backwards. Examples use brackets to group grammatical units.
