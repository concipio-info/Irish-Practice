This standalone app works by opening this HTML file in a browser. It needs no server, installation, internet connection or account. All lessons and exercises are inside this file.

Three ways to practise

  1. Choose: select the missing connector or connector-and-verb phrase.
  2. Type: supply the same phrase. Case, punctuation and extra spaces are ignored. Fadas matter by default; the accent buttons help.
  3. Build: click words in order to reproduce the supplied model sentence. Click a selected word to return it to the bank.

Use the English meaning and the supplied Irish sentence together. A gap may include the verb so that you practise mutations as well as the connector. Different Irish translations can be valid; typing and building check the displayed lesson’s model, not every possible translation. Connector-family hints make the target explicit.

What is covered?

Coordination, contrast, consequence, reason, time, open and hypothetical conditions, negative conditions, concession, purpose, precaution and indirect questions. Examples include present, habitual present, past, future and conditional verbs, positive and negative clauses, copular constructions and some verbal-noun clauses.

This is a broad practice collection, not an exhaustive grammar of every dialect, tense combination or literary construction. The exercises use mainly standard written Irish. Pronunciation hints are approximate English-friendly guides for connector phrases, with a Munster leaning; they cannot represent all dialect sounds. “Kh” represents the sound in Scottish loch. They are not recordings.

Key distinctions: má vs dá; when nuair a vs conditional má; reason “since” vs time “since”; conditional “if” vs indirect-question “whether”; independent mar tá vs dependent toisc go bhfuil.
