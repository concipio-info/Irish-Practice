How Irish questions fit together

Standard written forms, with common alternatives noted. Brackets show groups that work together. A broad course in the main modern patterns—not every possible sentence or regional variant. The conditional is a mood; the imperative is a command, not a question tense.

Typing and accepted alternatives

Fadas matter. Capital letters, repeated spaces, question marks and straight/curly apostrophes do not affect marking. Some common alternatives are accepted, but this is a model-answer trainer, not a general Irish grammar checker. An unrecognised valid translation may need comparison with the model.

Tile mode practises word order with already inflected words. Type mode also tests particles, mutations and spelling. A hint makes the item “assisted”; revealing an answer or skipping it adds it to mistake review. Scores count unaided correct answers only.

Scope

Includes neutral and negative yes/no questions; present, present habitual, past, past habitual, future and conditional; all eleven commonly taught irregular verbs; information questions; copula and tags; prepositional and embedded questions; open and hypothetical conditions; progressive, completed-action and autonomous constructions. “All question words” includes the productive families and common variants in the reference table; compounds and dialectal expressions are not a closed list.

Question words & pronunciation

Open a Teanglann entry and choose Pronunciation. Choose Munster where a recording is available; some particles and inflected forms have no individual recording. Word recordings do not represent the sound of a whole connected sentence.

For a full question, copy the revealed answer and paste it into ABAIR, then select a Munster voice if available. ABAIR produces synthetic speech. Pronunciation resources need internet; the exercises work offline.
