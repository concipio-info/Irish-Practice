Tá or Is?

Choose the Irish “to be” that fits the meaning.

☘
Tá

A state, place, or action

Use a form of bí with an adjective, a location, or an ongoing action. Possession often uses ag.

Tá mé tuirseach. I am tired.
Is

A category or identity

Use the copula to classify someone or identify who or what they are. It also appears in several set expressions.

Is múinteoir í. She is a teacher.
Useful nuance: “Is dochtúir é” and “Tá sé ina dhochtúir” can both mean “He is a doctor”. The second uses the separate ina + noun construction. Judge each sentence by its whole pattern, rather than by the English “is” alone.

Remember the pattern

  • Tá + adjective: Tá an tae te (the tea is hot).
  • Tá + place: Tá an leabhar ar an mbord (the book is on the table).
  • Is + noun: Is ceoltóir í (she is a musician).
  • Is + identity: Is í Máire an múinteoir (Máire is the teacher).
  • Special expressions: Is maith liom (I like), Is as Corcaigh mé (I am from Cork).

Scope: present affirmative sentences. Questions, negatives and past forms introduce more forms than tá and is.

Grammar reference: An Caighdeán Oifigiúil, Chapter 7 (copula) and the learner's guide to the copula.
